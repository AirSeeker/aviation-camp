## Introduction

Primary and intermediate flight training focuses on building and refining basic airmanship skills. Learning to fly is often compared to driving an automobile; however, this comparison is misleading. Aircraft operate within a three-dimensional environment that requires heightened sensory awareness, precise control coordination, and continuous risk management.

Airmanship represents more than mechanical proficiency. It encompasses a deep understanding of flight principles, the ability to operate an aircraft with precision both on the ground and in flight, and the application of sound aeronautical judgment to maximize operational safety.

<img src="/images/AFH/ch01/img_p22_1.png" alt="Progression of primary and intermediate flight training from pre-solo to checkride" />

The ultimate goal of flight training is to produce a safe, competent pilot. Successfully passing the practical test for pilot certification is merely incidental to achieving that primary objective.

---

## Core Concepts of Airmanship

Developing sound airmanship requires dedicated effort from both the learner and the flight instructor from the very first flight. Flight training teaches foundational motor skills that allow a pilot to respond intuitively to aircraft feedback:

* **Coordination:** The ability to use the hands and feet together subconsciously and in the proper relationship to control Pitch, Bank, and Yaw, producing desired results without overcontrolling.
* **Timing:** The application of muscular coordination at the exact instant required to make maneuvers smooth, steady, and continuous.
* **Control Touch:** The tactile sensitivity to feel the action of the airplane through varying pressures and aerodynamic resistance transmitted to the flight controls, allowing the pilot to immediately assess and anticipate changes in attitude and speed.
* **Speed Sense:** The conscious and subconscious ability to detect and react promptly to variations in Indicated Airspeed (IAS).

<img src="/images/AFH/ch01/img_p23_2.png" alt="Airmanship principles and aircraft operations" />

> **Attention:** Flight training does not aim to master only one specific make and model. Its purpose is to develop safe habits, foundational knowledge, and control techniques that transfer seamlessly to complex, high-performance aircraft.

## The Role of the FAA and Regulatory Structure

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) is empowered by the U.S. Congress to promote civil aviation safety by establishing standards for airmen, aircraft certification, and operational rules. These standards are codified in **Title 14 of the Code of Federal Regulations (14 CFR)**, titled *Aeronautics and Space*.

<img src="/images/AFH/ch01/img_p24_3.png" alt="Title 14 CFR Chapter 1 organization and subchapters" />

Title 14 CFR is divided into subchapters and numbered parts:

### Key Parts Relevant to Flight Operations and Airworthiness

* **14 CFR Part 21:** Prescribes procedural requirements for issuing airworthiness certificates and approvals for products and parts. A **Standard Airworthiness Certificate (FAA Form 8100-2)** must be displayed in the aircraft pursuant to 14 CFR Part 91, § 91.203(b). It remains valid as long as the aircraft conforms to its approved type design and is maintained in a condition for safe operation.
* **14 CFR Part 39:** Details the authority and procedures for issuing **Airworthiness Directives (ADs)**. ADs are legally enforceable rules issued when an unsafe condition is found in an aircraft, engine, propeller, or appliance, and that condition is likely to exist or develop in other products of the same type design.
* **14 CFR Part 43:** Governs maintenance, preventive maintenance, rebuilding, and alterations performed on aircraft holding a U.S. airworthiness certificate.
* **14 CFR Part 45:** Prescribes identification details, including nationality markings, registration numbers, and data plates for aircraft, engines, and propellers.
* **14 CFR Part 61:** Governs the certification requirements, privileges, and operational limitations for pilots, flight instructors, and ground instructors.
* **14 CFR Part 91:** Contains general operating and flight rules for operations under Visual Flight Rules (VFR) and Instrument Flight Rules (IFR), as well as general maintenance and equipment mandates.

> **Attention:** Advisory guidance published in FAA handbooks is not legally binding in its own right and does not serve as an independent basis for enforcement action. However, adhering to FAA-approved operating procedures establishes standard industry practices for safe flight.

## Practical Application

An accomplished pilot consistently exhibits analytical skill and disciplined execution:

1. **Situational Assessment:** Accurately evaluates changing circumstances in real time and selects the proper procedure.
2. **Predictive Judgment:** Projects the outcome of a given action or set of environmental conditions before executing a maneuver.
3. **Aircraft Performance Awareness:** Continuously gauges the operational capabilities and limitations of the aircraft under prevailing conditions.
4. **Continuous Risk Mitigation:** Proactively identifies hazards, assesses the associated risk, and implements appropriate mitigation strategies throughout every phase of flight.

---

## Safety Considerations

Primary flight training instills habits that prevent operational errors. Operating within personal and aircraft limits is fundamental to survival in aviation.

> **Attention:** Safe pilots recognize their personal minimums and operational limitations. Never allow external pressures, such as schedule deadlines or passenger expectations, to influence you to exceed your training, proficiency, or aircraft capabilities.

---

## Summary

* **Airmanship** combines aerodynamic knowledge, motor control skills, and sound judgment to ensure operational safety and efficiency.
* Four key sensory and motor skills form the foundation of flight control: **coordination**, **timing**, **control touch**, and **speed sense**.
* Skills developed during primary training are transferable across diverse aircraft makes and models.
* The FAA enforces safety through **Title 14 CFR**, with specific parts governing pilot certification (Part 61), aircraft airworthiness and directives (Parts 21 and 39), maintenance standards (Part 43), and operational rules (Part 91).
* A Standard Airworthiness Certificate remains valid only while the aircraft is maintained in accordance with approved type design and applicable regulations.
